Is every sample from the person on its label?

Tubes get swapped, labels get typed wrong, and sample sheets slip by a row. In a study where each person is sampled more than once, one mix-up quietly breaks every paired test. SwapSeq reads each person's microbial fingerprint and points to the samples that belong to someone else.

Results

How it works

Three checks, all run in your browser on your own table.

A fingerprint of rare taxa

Everyone carries a few uncommon ASVs that stay with them week after week. SwapSeq compares which ASVs each sample holds, at a matched sequencing depth, and their log-ratio profile. A sample closer to somebody else than to its own person stands out.

A test for swapped pairs

Two swapped tubes leave a signature: tube A fits person B and tube B fits person A. SwapSeq tests every pair and names the partner when swapping their labels back makes both fit again.

Calibrated on your own table

Before judging, SwapSeq swaps hundreds of labels in a copy of your data to learn what an error looks like there. It then gives strong flags with almost no false alarms, a list to check against lab records, and an estimate of how many swaps it would catch in your study.

Tested on public studies

Known errors were planted in real published tables at about a 1% rate, 30 to 60 times each, and every result was counted. Bray-Curtis is the same pipeline with the distance used by a recent shotgun mislabel detector.

Before you trust a flag

  • Two or more samples per person. Three or more works better. With exactly two, SwapSeq can say which two people do not fit, but not always which tube moved.
  • People who live together share microbes. Add a household column and look-alikes from one home move to the check list.
  • Infants and same-donor animals carry little individual signal. SwapSeq says so with a low power estimate and rarely flags anything.
  • Antibiotics and illness can make a sample unlike its owner without making it like anyone else. Those usually stay unflagged.
  • A flag is a reason to check the lab records. Look at plate maps, extraction batches and sample sheets before you relabel or drop anything.
  • Your data stays on your computer. Opening a file here reads it inside your browser. No server ever receives it.

Privacy. SwapSeq has no server. Files you open are read by your browser and never sent anywhere. This page sets no cookies and uses no analytics or tracking. The website host may keep standard access logs, such as IP addresses, for security.
